Large language models can give decision-makers access to optimization through interactive agents that propose, interpret, and refine solutions, but conversations are much harder to evaluate than one-shot approaches. We build LLM-powered decision agents that role-play diverse stakeholders, each governed by an internal utility function but communicating like a real decision-maker, and use them to evaluate optimization agents at scale.

In a school scheduling case study, the same optimization agent converges to much higher-quality solutions through conversation than one-shot evaluation would suggest. Tailored optimization agents, with domain-specific prompts and structured tools, reach better solutions in fewer interactions than general-purpose chatbots.
