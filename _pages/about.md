Hi! I am Joshua Drossman, a PhD candidate at MIT's Operations Research Center, advised by Prof. Alexandre Jacquillat. My research interests lie at the intersection of machine learning and optimization, with a particular focus on the interplay between Large Language Models and optimization.

My current work spans two directions:

* **LLM agents for interactive optimization.** I design LLM agents that help decision-makers formulate and solve optimization problems through conversation, and develop scalable methods for evaluating them using simulated stakeholders ([paper](/publication/2026-04-03-lets-have-a-conversation)).
* **Large-scale optimization for election planning.** I build mixed-integer optimization models for polling-place location, voter assignment, and precinct redistricting, and develop exact decomposition methods that solve instances 100x larger than previous approaches. In five Montana counties, these models reduced average voter travel burden by 5–12%.

Before MIT, I spent two years at Wood Mackenzie as a Supply Chain Analyst (promoted to Senior Analyst), building forecasting models and data tools for utility, oil & gas, and natural resources clients. I received my B.S.E. in Operations Research and Financial Engineering from Princeton University in 2022, graduating magna cum laude, with a senior thesis on optimizing CO₂ capture, transport, and storage infrastructure under uncertainty.
